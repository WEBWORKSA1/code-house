Why links rather than embeds

Videos are linked rather than embedded for two reasons. Embeds load third-party scripts and cookies before you have decided you want them, which slows the page and expands what is tracked. And video URLs rot — channels delete and re-upload, and an embedded dead video is worse than a search that always works. Links open on YouTube, where the creator gets the view and the credit.

All videos found through these links are published by their own creators on YouTube and are subject to YouTube's terms. Code.House is not affiliated with any of the channels and earns nothing from your watching them.

How to learn from video without wasting the time

  1. Watch once at speed, then build. Passive watching feels productive and retains almost nothing. The retention comes from typing it yourself afterwards, from memory, and getting stuck.
  2. Pause and predict. Before the instructor writes the next line, guess what it will be. Being wrong is the useful part.
  3. Change the project. Build the course project with a different subject than the one demonstrated. It forces you to understand rather than transcribe.
  4. Check the date. A three-year-old framework course will teach patterns that have since been replaced. Language fundamentals age far better than framework specifics.
  5. Stop when it stops teaching. Finishing a ten-hour course is not an achievement in itself. Leave when you can build the thing.
